## Slide 1: Cover  (about 16 s)

My name is Rashedul Arefin Ifty, from the Dependable AI Systems Laboratory. My supervisor is Associate Professor Fumio Machida. In this presentation, I report an empirical analysis of entangled enhancement in an autonomous driving pipeline.

## Slide 2: Outline  (about 8 s)

The presentation has five parts. They are the background, the objective, the setup, the results, and the conclusion.

## Slide 3: Background: Entangled Enhancement  (about 63 s)

A machine learning system, or MLS, connects several ML models sequentially. Each model uses the output of the upstream model before it. In our pipeline, the perception model C1 finds the objects, the prediction model C2 predicts their paths, and the planning model C3 plans the path of the vehicle. Retraining C1 gives the updated model C1 prime. Wang and Machida define entangled enhancement as a retraining that improves one ML model but fails to improve the whole MLS. Two causes can produce it. In the first cause, a downstream model is trained on the output of the original upstream model, including its errors. The downstream model learns to work with these errors. The retraining changes these errors. In the second cause, the updated model improves on average but becomes worse on the inputs that matter downstream.

## Slide 4: Existing Studies and Their Limitation  (about 55 s)

Sculley et al. describe how a change to one model affects every later model, but not how often this effect occurs. Wu et al. show that improving one model can make the whole system worse, but not in a driving pipeline. Wang and Machida model when to retrain each model. Their models need the probability of entangled enhancement, which nobody has measured in a driving pipeline. Xu et al. show that the prediction error increases when the input comes from a real perception model instead of the ground truth, which is the objects labelled by hand. They do not study a retraining. No study therefore measures how often entangled enhancement occurs in a driving pipeline, or which cause produces it.

## Slide 5: Objective and Research Questions  (about 61 s)

In this study, we empirically investigate whether a retraining that improves the perception model C1 increases the error of the prediction model C2 and the planning model C3. We ask three research questions. RQ1 asks whether the error increases, because the models of Wang and Machida need this probability. H1 states that the error can increase. RQ2 asks whether the increase comes from the adaptation of C2 and C3 to the errors of C1. H2 states that the increase is larger after training on the output of C1 than after training on the ground truth. RQ3 asks whether we can detect entangled enhancement without ground truth, because new data have no ground truth after deployment. H3 states that retrainings with entangled enhancement change the outputs of C1 and C2 more than other retrainings.

## Slide 6: Target Pipeline and Notation  (about 59 s)

We use YOLOv11n as the perception model C1. C1 finds the objects in the front-camera image. We employ an AutoBot-Ego style transformer as the prediction model C2. C2 reads the objects of the last 1.5 seconds and outputs six possible paths of three seconds for each object. For the planning model C3, we employ a PlanT style transformer. C3 receives the objects with their most likely path, the driving command, and the ego path. The ego path is the path of the ego vehicle, the vehicle that carries the camera, over the last 1.5 seconds. For each retraining, delta one is the gain of C1 in accuracy. Delta two is the increase of the prediction error. Delta three is the increase of the planning error at three seconds.

## Slide 7: Two Training Modes of C₂ and C₃  (about 54 s)

To answer RQ2, we train C2 and C3 in two modes. In GT training, where GT means ground truth, C2 and C3 learn from the objects labelled by hand. They never see the errors of C1. In C1-output training, C2 and C3 learn from the output of C1, including its errors. In both modes, we train C2 and C3 once and keep them fixed. After each retraining, both versions receive the output of the same C1 prime. Only the training differs. If the error increases only after C1-output training, the cause is the adaptation to the errors of C1. If the error increases after both modes, the increase points to the uneven improvement of C1 prime.

## Slide 8: Experimental Setup  (about 45 s)

We use 350 of the 850 scenes of nuScenes. We split the scenes into four parts by city. Boston 1 trains C1. Boston 2 trains C2 and C3, which therefore see the real errors of C1 on unseen images. Singapore 1 is used for the retraining of C1, as in an update after deployment in a new city. Singapore 2 is the test set with 1,720 test samples. We train two original C1 with the random seeds 0 and 1. We retrain each original C1 with 15 settings of data amount, epochs and seed. This gives 30 retrainings.

## Slide 9: Evaluation Procedure  (about 59 s)

For each retraining, we run the pipeline with the original C1 and with C1 prime on the same 50 test scenes, with C2 and C3 fixed. The differences give the three deltas. A single positive delta can come from chance. We therefore count the increases at three levels. Level one counts any increase. Level two counts increases larger than the seed variation. The two original C1 differ only in the random seed and have almost the same accuracy. Replacing one by the other still changes the error by up to 0.112 metres for prediction and 0.018 metres for planning. A smaller increase can therefore come from randomness. Level three resamples the test scenes 2,000 times. The middle 95 percent of the resampled increases must lie above zero.

## Slide 10: Check of C₂ and C₃  (about 45 s)

Before the results, we check C2 and C3. If they were no better than a simple rule, a change of their error would say nothing about the retraining. C2 has an error of 0.77 metres on ground-truth objects, against 4.48 metres when every object stays still. C3 without the ego path is not better than the average Boston path. We therefore do not use this variant. C3 with the ego path has about 4 metres, against 9.25 metres. Removing the objects still moves the plan of C3 by 0.73 to 1.65 metres. C3 therefore uses the objects.

## Slide 11: Result: Prediction Model C₂  (about 53 s)

C1 prime is more accurate than C1 in all 30 retrainings. The figure shows delta two, the increase of the prediction error, for each retraining. The navy dots show C1-output training, and the gold diamonds show GT training. After C1-output training, delta two is positive in 23 retrainings, above the seed variation in 11, and significant in 3. After GT training, the counts are 5, 0 and 0. In all 30 retrainings, delta two is larger after C1-output training, although both versions of C2 receive the same input. All 11 clear increases come from the original C1 with seed 0, on the left half. As a result, we confirm H1 and H2 for C2.

## Slide 12: Result: Planning Model C₃  (about 49 s)

Next, we test whether the increase reaches the planning model C3. The figure shows delta three, the increase of the planning error. After C1-output training, delta three is positive in 18 retrainings, above the seed variation in 13, and significant in 1. After GT training, the counts are 29, 29 and 10. The increase is small, at most 0.13 metres against a planning error of about 4 metres. Most of the plan of C3 comes from the ego path. The increase is also more frequent after GT training. A GT-trained C3 cannot have adapted to the errors of C1. The first cause therefore cannot explain this increase.

## Slide 13: Result: Retraining C₂ and C₃ on the Output of C₁′  (about 48 s)

If the first cause is right, retraining C2 and C3 on the output of C1 prime should help. We test this idea on one retraining of each original C1. The red bars show the errors after the retraining with C2 and C3 fixed. The navy bars show the errors after we retrain C2 and C3. The prediction error decreases by 0.36 and 0.16 metres. The planning error decreases by 0.44 and 0.30 metres. Both errors fall below their values before the retraining. This result supports the first cause. However, retraining every downstream model after every update has a cost. This cost motivates RQ3.

## Slide 14: Result: Detection Without Ground Truth  (about 54 s)

For RQ3, we test nine measures that compare the outputs before and after the retraining on the same images. Seven use the output of C1, such as the number of objects per image. Two use the output of C2, such as the shift of the predicted positions. A useful measure is larger when delta two, the increase of the prediction error, is larger. Rank correlation measures this agreement. A value of one is perfect, and zero is none. The best measure, the shift of the predicted positions, reaches 0.68 over all retrainings, but only 0.29 and minus 0.28 within each original C1. The measure separates the two original C1, not single retrainings. H3 is therefore not supported.

## Slide 15: Conclusion  (about 38 s)

In this study, we investigated entangled enhancement in an autonomous driving pipeline of three ML models. For RQ1, we observe that a retraining that improves C1 increases the prediction error of C2, clearly in 11 of 30 retrainings. The planning error increases by at most 0.13 metres. For RQ2, we confirm that the increase in C2 comes from the adaptation of C2 to the errors of C1. For RQ3, we find no measure without ground truth that identifies single retrainings with entangled enhancement.

## Slide 16: Future Work  (about 32 s)

Our next step is the early detection of entangled enhancement, before C1 prime is deployed and before ground truth exists. We also plan more test scenes, because only 3 of the 30 increases are significant with 50 scenes. We plan more original C1, because all 11 clear increases come from one original C1. Finally, the measured frequency can serve as the probability in the models of Wang and Machida.

# Expected Questions

Short answers for the discussion. Every number comes from the results shown in the slides or the appendix.

**Q1. What explains the increase of the planning error after GT training?**

The first cause cannot explain it. A GT-trained C3 learns from the true objects and their true future paths. At test time, the same C3 receives the detected objects and the paths that C2 predicts. A change in the objects that C1 prime finds can therefore move the plan of this C3 more. This study does not test this explanation directly, and the increase is at most 0.13 metres.

**Q2. Why do all clear increases come from the original C1 with seed 0?**

The C2 trained on the output of this C1 is sensitive to a change in the errors of C1. When we replace this C1 by the other original C1, which is almost equally accurate (0.009 apart in mAP50), the error of this C2 increases by 0.112 metres. The C2 trained on the output of the seed-1 C1 improves by 0.098 metres under the same replacement. The adaptation of C2 therefore differs between the two original C1.

**Q3. Two measures on slide 14 correlate well within seed 0. Why is H3 still not supported?**

Within seed 0, the number of objects per image reaches plus 0.58, and the largest gap of the confidence values reaches minus 0.56. Within seed 1, the same measures reach only minus 0.14 and minus 0.09. A useful warning sign must work for every original C1, and 15 retrainings per original C1 are a small sample.

**Q4. Does the ego path let C3 ignore the objects?**

An ego-path input can let a planning model ignore the objects on nuScenes, which Li et al. and Zhai et al. report. Without the ego path, C3 is not better than the average Boston path. With the ego path, removing the objects still moves the plan by 0.73 to 1.65 metres, which is above our threshold of 0.5 metres.

**Q5. Why is the accuracy of C1 low, with an mAP50 of about 0.11?**

C1 is trained on only 100 Boston scenes with the front camera, and the test scenes come from another city. This study needs the change of accuracy, not a high accuracy. Every one of the 30 retrainings improves mAP50 by 0.048 to 0.118.

**Q6. Why only 350 scenes and 50 test scenes?**

We reduced the data to 350 scenes to run 30 retrainings with two training modes on one GPU. With 50 test scenes, only 3 of the 30 increases are statistically significant. More test scenes are part of the future work.

**Q7. Does the accuracy gain delta one predict the increase?**

No. The rank correlation between delta one and delta two is plus 0.04. A larger accuracy gain does not come with a larger increase of the prediction error.
