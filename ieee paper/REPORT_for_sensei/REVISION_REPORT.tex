\section{Summary}
This report records the revisions made to the manuscript following the written
advice received in August 2026. The advice identified five recurring problems in
the presentation, namely inconsistent word choice, sentences carrying more than
one message, sentences too short to be understood, ambiguous pronouns, and
unclear subsection structure. Fifteen specific examples were named. All fifteen
have been addressed. The revision also covers further occurrences of the same
patterns that were found by searching the whole manuscript.

The manuscript builds without errors and remains within ten pages. No numerical
result, table entry, or claim was changed by this revision.

\section{Sentences Carrying More Than One Message}
The four sentences named in the advice were each split into shorter sentences,
with one point per sentence.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The last sentence of Section II-A, on how a change reaches a model that was
not retrained, is now three sentences.
\item The last sentence of Section IV-C, on the L2 metric and its caveat, is now
three sentences that separate what the protocol is, that it has known limitations,
and how it is used here.
\item The sentence in Section V-E on the predictability of the outcome is now two
sentences.
\item The sentence in Section V-G on the AUROC of the drift score is now two
sentences, and the value is stated together with what it means.
\item An additional sentence in Section III-A, on why all the changes are reported
together, was split in the same way.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Sentences That Lacked Information}
The four sentences named in the advice were expanded.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Fine-tuning data size.} The description now states what the half and
the quarter subsets are, how they are selected, that the same subsets are used in
every run, and why this factor is varied.
\item \textbf{The isolation check.} The claim that the check is exact is now
supported by the reasoning, namely that both models are deterministic and have no
trainable parameters, so identical inputs give identical outputs.
\item \textbf{Figure reference.} The sentence ``Fig.~2 shows the signature'' was
replaced. The values are now stated first and the figure is referenced afterwards.
\item \textbf{Divergence of the two metrics.} The sentence now states which metric
moved in which direction.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ambiguous Pronouns}
The three sentences named in the advice were rewritten so that every reference
names the thing it refers to.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The last sentence of Section II-B, which contained ``the two'' and ``it'',
now names entangled enhancement and ordinary regression explicitly.
\item The sentence in Section II-C beginning ``It is what lets us\ldots'' now
begins with ``Isolated mode''.
\item The sentence in Section V-H, which used ``it'' for two different things,
was rewritten as two sentences that name CARA and the disagreement between the two
metrics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Other Corrections}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The role of the drift score is now stated explicitly. It is a screening
statistic and not the admission criterion, and the admission decision uses the
change at the last model.
\item Grammatical corrections were made throughout the abstract, the introduction,
and Section II, including subject-verb agreement, article use, and the consistent
spelling of ``entangled enhancement''.
\item Redundant passages were removed from Sections V-H, V-I, VI-B, and the
conclusion, where the same point was made twice.
\end{itemize}
